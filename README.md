# Hairtrigger: what being second into a pool costs

**Live demo:** [vishal5208.github.io/hairtrigger/](https://vishal5208.github.io/hairtrigger/)

One trade, executed twice on the same price gap: once the moment the Reactive callback lands, once held to a contract-enforced 7-second gate. Over 28 rounds the fast fill was better priced in 28 of 28.

At a 162 bps gap the 2-second fill netted +72 bps and the later fill netted −30 bps on the identical trade. Fitted across all 28 rounds, break-even sits at about 91 bps of detected gap for the fast fill and about 206 bps for the later fill — but the fast figure is an extrapolation: the reactive only fires above its 150 bps threshold, and the smallest gap this run measured was 162 bps. The 115 bps between those floors is the second trader's cost — the price impact the first fill leaves behind, not a function of the seconds elapsed. The destination pool is idle between the fast fill and the control fill, so a copy landing at 9 s or 12 s would face the same price: the gap is the first fill's footprint, and speed is what lets the fast arm be the one that leaves it.

**Disclosed up front:** the pools are seeded by us, the flow that opens each gap is generated by us, and the +7 s arm is our own control — no third-party flow exists on these testnets. Every number is regenerated by the `analysis/` scripts: Version 1 by `table.py` and `breakeven.py` from `data/rounds.csv`, Version 2 by `sizing.py` from `data/v2-rounds.csv`, and the delivery floor by `pingfloor.py`.

> **Two versions.** Everything above and through "The second-trader counterfactual" is **Version 1**: a completed 28-round latency experiment with a *fixed* 0.0004 WETH trade, whose flat trade size is what makes the linear break-even (91 / 206 bps) well defined. **Version 2** replaces the fixed trade with a profit-maximising one sized per gap; it is **deployed and measured over 23 cross-chain rounds** (see "Version 2 — net-optimal sizing" below). Version 1 data is frozen under `data/archive/fixed-size/`; the deployed addresses in the next section are the **Version 2** contracts, with the Version 1 deployment listed there for provenance.

### The delivery floor — what the network costs with no contract logic

To separate the network's delivery cost from our contract's, a second reactive contract was deployed that does nothing but request a trivial callback to a no-op executor on the same destination (Unichain Sepolia). It subscribes to plain ERC20 `Transfer` events on Base Sepolia; the callback payload carries no trading logic and the executor only emits a timestamped event. Over 20 deliveries (`analysis/pingfloor.py`, `PingReactive` `0xf6b3cd0FA15d4eb29719e68489DAd8A645a8f202` on Lasna, `PingExecutor` `0xf10502b11891a5188972917f3f25c6f469ff071c` on Unichain):

| leg | median | min | max |
|---|---|---|---|
| callback (Lasna reaction → delivery) | 3 s | 2 s | 4 s |
| end-to-end (origin block → delivery) | 2 s | 2 s | 3 s |
| message (origin block → Lasna reaction) | −1 s | −1 s | 0 s |

These are the arb callbacks' numbers: callback leg median 3 s, end-to-end median 2 s. A callback that performs no contract work arrives in the same time as the one that executes the trade, so the 2–3 seconds is relayer transit plus destination block inclusion — the floor of that path in our runs on Lasna. The no-op also ran at a 200,000 gas limit against the arb callback's 400,000 and delivered in the same time. Reactive's own documentation states that a higher `gasLimit` does not accelerate delivery or increase priority, and the callback configuration carries only `chainId`, `recipient`, `gasLimit` and `payload`, with no priority field ([dev.reactive.network](https://dev.reactive.network/economy)); the spec says it, and these 20 deliveries confirm it. Gas remaining at delivery was essentially constant (22,888, two trials at 22,899 of the forwarded budget), as expected for a fixed payload. The message leg again reads −1..0 s, the same whole-second clock quantization described in the limitations.

## How it works

One contract observes, decides, and requests the fill; a keeper enforces the +7 s counterfactual by replaying the identical trade.

```
Base Sepolia (84532)                     Lasna (5318007)                  Unichain Sepolia (1301)
┌─────────────────────┐              ┌──────────────────────────┐       ┌──────────────────────┐
│ MockPool WETH/HTRG  │──Swap log──▶│ HairtriggerReactive      │──────▶│ ArbExecutor (fast)   │
│ MockPool WETH/HTRG  │◀─Swap log───│  · last price per chain  │       │ ArbExecutor (control)│
└─────────────────────┘              │  · gap in bps, cooldown  │       │  · same pool, +7s    │
          ▲                          │  · requestCallbackV_1_0  │       └──────────┬───────────┘
          │                          └──────────────────────────┘                  │
   Flow.s.sol (disclosed)                                                            ▼
                                                                              Fill event → keeper → csv
```

- Subscriptions are registered in the reactive constructor, one per chain; the constructor takes arrays of chain IDs and pools, so it watches **N venues**, not a hardcoded pair.
- The reactive stores the last `sqrtPriceX96` observed per venue and, on every matching swap, re-scans all venues rather than comparing a fixed pair.
- The destination is the venue with the highest `sqrtPriceX96` (where 1 WETH buys the most HTRG) **among the venues that can execute** (have an executor and non-zero reserves). A cheaper venue without an executor is used only as a price reference, never traded on.
- The reference price is the dearest observed venue. With two venues this reduces exactly to "compare the two and buy on the cheaper one".
- Gap is computed relative to the cheaper venue: `((hi/lo)^2 - 1) * 10,000` bps.
- The callback payload is `onArb(address,uint256,uint256,uint256,uint64)`; the first argument is the `address(0)` placeholder that the system replaces with the reactive contract's address for authentication.

The executor's own fills emit `Swap` logs too, so a large gap that survives its first fill can trigger a second round after the cooldown. Those rounds are included and are indistinguishable from flow-triggered ones in every respect except the `origin_tx` column, which identifies the triggering transaction.

## The experiment

Each gap produces two real on-chain fills on the cheaper venue, in this order:

1. **Fast arm** — the reactive observes a `Swap` on the origin chain, computes the cross-chain gap, and requests a callback. The executor on the cheaper venue fills as soon as the callback lands; the measured end-to-end latency is reported per round, not assumed.
2. **Control arm** — the keeper waits until the destination chain's block timestamp is at least 7 seconds past detection, then calls `runControl()` with the *same* `roundId`, `amountIn`, and `detectedAt` from the original `GapDetected`. It executes the identical trade, unconditionally — it does not re-detect the gap.

The control arm does not share the fast arm's slippage guard (it fills with `minAmountOut = 0`); if it did, its outcome would be a deterministic function of the slippage tolerance, not a measurement. `gap_still_open_at_7s` is `true` when the control's realized net profit is positive.

One bias, stated up front: because we are the only participant, the fast arm's own fill is part of the pool state the control faces, so the control is measured against a *slower copy of ourselves* that trades after we did — conservative for the control. This is the whole effect: the destination pool is idle between the fast fill and the control fill (the keeper's `runControl` is the only swapper in that window), so the gap is the **first fill's footprint**, not a function of how many seconds the copy waits. A copy landing at 9 s or 12 s faces the same price. Read it as "the second trader into this pool, after the fast fill has already moved it," not as a claim about external competition.

Parameters (Version 1): `thresholdBps = 150`, `amountIn = 0.0004 WETH` (0.8% of the seed), `slippageBps = 300`, `cooldown = 30s`, `controlDelay = 7s`. Version 2 replaces `amountIn` with a per-gap `minTradeWeth` / `maxTradeWeth` range and sizes each trade (see below). The seed is **0.05 WETH / 1,000 HTRG** per pool; flow was sized at 1.2%–2.8% of the seed (0.0006–0.0014 WETH), which opens gaps of several hundred bps. Gap percentages are scale-invariant if flow and trade stay at those fractions of the seed, and absolute profit scales with the seed, but gas does not: at 0.05 WETH a high-hundreds-bps edge is still many times the ~2e-7 ETH Base-Sepolia fill cost.

One sizing consequence is visible in the results and is deliberate: the two arms together can only push `2 × amountIn = 0.0008 WETH` of flow back into the pool per round, so any single round with flow **above** 0.0008 WETH leaves the gap larger than it started (one 0.0004 fill moves a pool ~150 bps). Rounds 4–14 used 0.0008–0.0014 WETH and the gap grew round over round; rounds 15–32 used 0.0006 WETH, below the arber's fill capacity, and the gap shrank steadily from 1361 bps at round 15 to 162 bps at round 32 — at which point it had closed far enough that the 7 s-late control netted a loss. What the experiment isolates is not gap-closure but the **first fill's footprint**: the same 0.0004 WETH trade fills at a strictly better price when it is first into the pool than when it is the second entry behind our own fast fill, in every round — and because the destination pool is idle in between, the size of that footprint does not depend on how many seconds the second entry waits.

## What is measured, and how

Let `sqrtPriceX96 = sqrt(HTRG per WETH) * 2^96`. Higher means HTRG is cheaper.

| Column | Definition |
|---|---|
| `gap_at_detection_bps` | Gap from the reactive's `GapDetected` event, relative to the cheaper venue |
| `gap_at_fill_bps` | Signed gap between the origin venue's post-swap price and the destination's post-fill price. Positive: the destination is still cheaper |
| `latency_msg_sec` | Lasna reaction block timestamp - origin swap block timestamp: how fast the reactive saw the swap |
| `latency_callback_sec` | Destination fill block timestamp - Lasna reaction block timestamp: callback delivery plus execution |
| `latency_end_to_end_sec` | Destination fill block timestamp - origin swap block timestamp, the headline number |
| `gas_used` | Gas of the fill transaction on the destination chain |
| `gas_cost_eth` | `gasUsed * effectiveGasPrice` |
| `callback_cost_rnk` | Drop in the reactive's Lasna liability (`balance - SYSTEM.debt(reactive)`) across the round: reactive execution plus the callback request |
| `gross_profit_eth` | `amountOut_HTRG * (2^96 / originSqrtPriceX96)^2 - amountIn_WETH` |
| `net_profit_eth` | `gross_profit_eth - gas_cost_eth` |
| `gap_still_open_at_7s` | `true` iff the control arm's `net_profit_eth > 0` |

The CSV is long-format, one row per arm per round keyed by `round_id`, so the two arms pair by round id and nothing else.

Compute is not the bottleneck. 25 of the 28 fast fills consumed exactly 128,429 gas and the other 3 consumed 128,465 — a 36-gas range, 32% of the 400,000 `gasLimit` in the `CallbackRequest`. `gasLimit` is a ceiling on what the callback may burn, not a bid for inclusion, so callback cost is constant by construction. The 2 to 3 second callback leg is therefore relayer transit plus destination block inclusion, not contract execution. The one remaining uncertainty is the origin-to-Lasna leg, which resolves to -1 to 0 seconds at whole-second resolution.

Three limits, stated plainly:

- **Timestamps are whole-second, and the msg leg is clock quantization.** Base and Unichain produce sub-second blocks, but EVM block timestamps are whole seconds, so every latency is quantized to ±1 s; a one-second fill and a 1.9-second fill are indistinguishable on-chain, and we report what the chains report. We do not report a separate "reactive decision" latency: `requestCallbackV_1_0` is called inside `react()`, so `GapDetected` and the system's `CallbackRequest` land in the same Lasna block and their difference is identically zero — the decision is synchronous and sub-block, and the only measurable legs are message delivery and callback execution. The origin→Lasna observation leg reads **−1 to 0 s in all 28 rounds**, which cannot be true elapsed time; it is quantization between the two chains' unsynchronized clocks, not a negative latency. That skew is also why the callback sub-leg (3 s) can read one second *above* the full end-to-end span (2 s): the sub-leg is counted from a start point that rounds to about one second after the origin swap. `msg + callback = end_to_end` holds exactly in all 28 fills; we do not promote the sub-leg over the whole span.
- **Profit is mark-to-market.** The HTRG is bought on the destination and valued at the origin venue's post-swap spot price. Nothing is sold and nothing crosses a bridge; the HTRG tokens on the two chains are independent deployments. The experiment measures the first fill's footprint on the second entry — the cost of being second into the pool — not whether a cross-chain round trip settles.
- **RNK is not converted to ETH.** It is a different asset and there is no testnet market rate. It is reported as its own column rather than folded into `net_profit_eth`.

### Summary

- Rounds: 28
- Message leg (origin swap → Lasna reaction): sub-second — observed block-time delta -1..0s is ±1s block quantization plus unsynchronized cross-chain clocks, so it is reported as a bound, not a signed latency; the per-round origin and Lasna block numbers are the provenance
- Fast-arm end-to-end latency (origin swap block → destination fill block): median 2s, p90 3s (min 2s, max 3s), ±1s quantized
- Callback leg (Lasna reaction → fill): median 3s, p90 3s
- Control-arm end-to-end latency: median 12s, p90 14s (±1s quantized). Of this, 7s is the enforced gate; the fill lands a median ~6s (range 4–7s) *after* the gate opens. That surplus is the keeper's tx send + inclusion, not extra hold — the gate is checked on the destination clock (`detectedAt+7`) and Unichain mines 1s blocks, so a fill 4–7s late cannot be block time. It does not move the result: the destination pool is idle between the fast fill (~2s) and the control fill (~12s) — the keeper's `runControl` is the only swapper in that window — so the control's fill price is identical to a fill at exactly +7s. Surplus distribution across the 28 rounds: 4s×1, 5s×10, 6s×9, 7s×8.
- Fast arm net-positive: 28/28
- Control cleared costs (+7s): 27/28 (96%)
- Median fast net: 0.00002962 WETH
- Median control net: 0.000024363 WETH
- Median fast − median control: 0.000005257 WETH
- Median paired difference (fast − control): 0.000005345 WETH

**Reading it.** The paired difference column is the **first fill's footprint**: on the identical 0.0004 WETH trade, the arm that trades first fills at a strictly better price than the second entry behind it in every round — median **+0.0000053 WETH per trade, ≈134 bps of the trade, about 22% more than the copy behind it**. The destination pool is idle between the two fills, so this is the price impact of the first trade, not a decay that grows with the wait: a second entry at 9 s or 12 s sees the same price. The `gap_still_open_at_7s` column is `true` in 27 of 28 rounds. The single exception is round 32, where the converged gap was thin enough (162 bps at detection, 53 bps at the fast fill) that the second entry netted −30 bps against a +72 bps first fill: when the gap is small, the first fill's footprint alone is enough to flip the copy's sign. In the wide-gap rounds the residual stays open after 7 s because there is no third-party competition and the arber is deliberately dust-sized — that is the disclosed lower-bound caveat, not a refutation of the per-trade footprint measured here. The two seconds is the whole point; the callback leg (~3 s) is the bottleneck, and the observation leg is sub-second.

### The gap floor — where speed stops mattering

Gas is not the binding cost, and because the trade size is fixed it is also flat across the run: the arbitrage is a constant 0.0004 WETH on both arms, so gas is **4.82 bps of the trade on the fast arm** (128,429 gas) and **1.00 bps on the control** (80,266 gas) — the three control fills that paid a 3× base fee reach 3.01 bps. What sets the floor is the round-trip the fill must overcome: the 30 bps swap fee plus the price impact of the 0.0004 order itself. That is why the break-even is tens of bps, not single digits, and why "the trade covers gas" is almost never the real question.

Fitting `net_bps` against the **detected** gap across all 28 rounds (slope ≈ 1, so profit tracks the gap almost one-for-one) puts the break-even at:

| Arm | net = 0 at detected gap | smallest gap that actually netted |
|---|---:|---:|
| Fast (2 s) | ≈ 91 bps | 162 bps (+72 bps net) |
| Control (+7 s) | ≈ 206 bps | 218 bps (+23 bps net) |

The fast figure below 162 bps is an extrapolation — that is the smallest gap this run produced — but the crossover itself is measured, on consecutive rounds as the gap converged:

| Round | Detected gap | Fast net | Control net |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 27 | 460 bps | +365 bps | +252 bps |
| 28 | 397 bps | +303 bps | +192 bps |
| 29 | 336 bps | +243 bps | +134 bps |
| 30 | 277 bps | +185 bps | +78 bps |
| 31 | 218 bps | +128 bps | +23 bps |
| 32 | 162 bps | +72 bps | -30 bps |

So how small a gap is still worth taking depends on your deadline: **below ~200 bps of detected gap the +7 s fill stops covering costs, while the 2 s fill stays profitable down to roughly half that.** The ~115 bps between the two break-evens is what being first is worth in this pool — the footprint the first fill leaves for any second entry to pay. It is a falsifiable claim rather than a stopwatch reading: it names where the slower trade loses money, and round 32 is that round. Both figures are specific to this liquidity (0.05 WETH seed, fixed 0.0004 order); a larger order or a deeper pool raises the impact/fee line and the break-even with it, while the gap between the two arms is the first fill's footprint in this pool. (Both tables and the gas figures are emitted by `python3 analysis/breakeven.py` from the same CSV.)

## The second-trader counterfactual

The previous Reactive network had ~7s blocks. A reactive system built on it would see the gap roughly seven seconds late. This repo answers a concrete version of that question with a paired experiment instead of an estimate: for every gap it detects, it executes the same trade as soon as the callback lands, and again the moment the contract's `block.timestamp >= detectedAt + 7` gate opens, on the same venue, against the same pool state. The realized control-arm end-to-end latency is 11–14 s (median 12 s), because the 7 s gate is a minimum and block inclusion adds a few seconds on top. The fraction of rounds where the 7 s-late fill still clears costs is `gap_still_open_at_7s`. Because the destination pool is idle between the two fills, what this measures is the **first fill's footprint** — the cost of being the second trader — not a decay curve: the 7 s gate separates the arms, and a copy at 9 s or 12 s would face the same price.

## Version 2 — net-optimal sizing

Version 1 trades a constant 0.0004 WETH. That is wrong in both directions: at a 250 bps gap it buys far less HTRG than the profit-maximising size, and inside the two-fee band it still trades and loses the fee. Version 2 computes the trade size per gap, at detection, when both venue prices are known at once.

**The size.** Buying `x` WETH for HTRG on the cheaper venue (reserves `r0` WETH, `r1` HTRG) and marking the HTRG at the dear venue's price gives gross profit. Maximising it with the 0.3% fee `γ = 0.997` and the two sqrt prices (cheaper = `sDest`, dear = `sDear`) yields a closed form:

```
x* = r0 · ( sqrt(γ)·sDest − sDear ) / (γ · sDear)
```

computed in integer `Math.mulDiv` with `sqrtGammaQ96 = sqrt(γ)·2^96` precomputed in the constructor. If `sqrt(γ)·sDest ≤ sDear` (inside the fee band) the optimum is zero and **no callback is sent**. Additive costs — gas and the callback fee — do not move the argmax, so the reactive maximises gross and the executor's realised fill decides whether the round clears. The result is clamped to `[minTradeWeth, maxTradeWeth]`: the floor skips dust trades, the cap keeps a wild price from draining inventory.

**Reserves from one log.** The reactive recovers each venue's reserves from the V3 `Swap` event alone — `liquidity L = sqrt(r0·r1)` and `sqrtPrice S = sqrt(r1/r0)·2^96`, so `r0 = L·2^96/S` and `r1 = L·S/2^96` — and rebuilds them on every matching swap. No signed-delta tracking, no separate state to drift; each event re-anchors the position. The initial reserves are derived at deploy from the seed (`initialLiquidity = floor(sqrt(seedWeth·seedHtrg))`, the same value `MockPool` emits).

**Two bugs fixed in passing.** The old `minAmountOut` used the *spot* price and ignored the 30 bps fee and price impact; it is now the actual constant-product output for `x*`, less `slippageBps`. And the executor clamps `amountIn` to its WETH balance instead of reverting when inventory is short.

**No double-fill.** A single arbitrage is in flight at a time. On trigger the reactive sets `inFlight`; a new trigger is refused until either the executor's fast `Fill(roundId, arm=0)` is observed (the reactive subscribes to `Fill` on both executors and closes the matching round) or `inFlightTimeout` (default 30 s) releases a lost callback. Because the fast fill's own `Swap` on the destination pool lands *while* the window is open, it updates the venue price but cannot be misread as a fresh trigger; the `Fill` that follows closes the window. The executor also keeps `filled[roundId][arm]` and reverts `AlreadyFilled` on any duplicate delivery, so a replayed callback cannot fill twice and the fast/control arms stay independent.

**No phantom rounds.** The system call that requests a callback is the one place a round can silently vanish: if `requestCallbackV_1_0` reverts (unfunded RNK, system pause, bad configuration), the naive version still advanced the round counter, started the cooldown, opened the in-flight window and emitted `GapDetected` — so the keeper would wait up to 120 s for a fast fill that could never arrive. The reactive now previews the round id and commits nothing until the request is accepted: on a reject it emits `CallbackFailed`, keeps the price observation, and leaves the cooldown, in-flight window and round counter untouched so the next price tick retries immediately. (A destination-side revert — the callback is requested but the executor rejects it — is not visible from Lasna; the in-flight timeout and the keeper's poll timeout are the backstop for that case, not this check.)

**Why it is strictly better, proven offline.** `test/Hairtrigger.t.sol` shows both halves against the seeded pools, with no live run needed:

- `test_dynamicBeatsFixedAtWideGap` — at a wide gap `x*` exceeds the old 0.002 WETH test size and its gross profit is strictly greater.
- `test_fixedSizeOverTradesInsideFeeBand` — inside the two-fee band `x* = 0` (skip), while the fixed size has *negative* gross profit.

**N-venue routing.** The contract is not wired to a fixed pair. The constructor takes arrays of chain IDs and pools, and on every observed swap `_bestArb()` scans all of them: the destination is the highest-priced **executable** venue (cheapest HTRG where a trade can actually land), and the reference is the lowest-priced venue (dearest HTRG) used for marking. A cheaper venue that has no executor is treated as a read-only price source and never routed to — so adding a third or fourth pool to the watch list cannot route a trade into a venue with nowhere to execute. The scan is O(venues) per observed swap; for the two-venue deployment it is identical to comparing the pair. Covered by `test_nVenueSkipsCheapestWithoutExecutor` and `test_nVenueRoutesToCheapestWhenExecutable`.

**Config.** `MIN_TRADE_WETH` / `MAX_TRADE_WETH` replace the old `TRADE_WETH` in `DeployReactive.s.sol` and force a fresh deploy (the constructor changed). The live Version 2 deployment above uses `minTradeWeth = 1e14`, `maxTradeWeth = 2e16`. `analysis/sizing.py` reports how size tracks the detected gap; `analysis/breakeven.py` refuses dynamic data (its linear model only fits the fixed-size run, archived under `data/archive/fixed-size/`).

## What is mocked, and why

- **Pools.** `MockPool` is a constant-product pool with a 30 bps fee and a `seed()` function. It emits the canonical Uniswap V3 `Swap` signature, so the reactive contract decodes and reacts exactly as it would to a real V3 pool. It is not Uniswap. `token0` is always WETH and `token1` is always HTRG, without address sorting, so both chains have the same price direction.
- **Token.** `HairtriggerToken` is owner-mintable HTRG, deployed independently on each chain. There is no bridge.
- **Flow.** `script/Flow.s.sol` is a disclosed flow generator: it wraps ETH and swaps into one pool to open a gap. It is the only source of swaps.
- **Control arm.** The +7s execution is ours, run by `analysis/keeper.py`. It is not another participant.
- **Pricing.** Destination gas is paid in ETH and treated as WETH 1:1 (redeemable on both chains). RNK is not converted.

## Tests

```bash
forge test
```

Covers the canonical topic, pool price/fee/slippage behavior, gap firing and cooldown, authorization on both contracts, the seven-second gate, and the paired assertion that the delayed fill is strictly worse than the fast fill. The Version 2 sizing tests cover the closed-form optimum (zero inside the fee band, growing with the gap, clamped to the cap), fee-aware `minAmountOut`, reserve recovery from a single `Swap` log, and the two-sided proof that dynamic sizing beats a fixed size at wide gaps and skips inside the fee band where a fixed size loses money. The in-flight/replay tests cover the window blocking a second trigger, a control fill not closing it, the timeout releasing it, a duplicate fast callback reverting, and the fast/control arms staying independent for the same round.

Beyond the example-based cases the suite runs a fuzz campaign and a stateful invariant campaign:

- **Fuzz** (`testFuzz_*`, 256 runs each): the sizing optimum never exceeds `maxTradeWeth` or lands between zero and `minTradeWeth`; the optimum is always zero inside the two-fee band (~150 bps); a wider gap never sizes smaller; and `minAmountOut` is always at or below the naive spot estimate for reserves consistent with the observed price.
- **Invariants** (`Invariant.t.sol`, 128 runs × 64-depth over a handler that fuzzes Swap logs, Fill logs, clock advances and the kill switch): an open in-flight window always references the latest fired round and opens at that fire's timestamp; `roundCounter` and `lastFireAt` move together; there is never an in-flight window without a round; and a venue's recovered reserves are never zeroed by a malformed log.
- **Adversarial scenarios**: a forged/malformed Swap log with `sqrtPriceX96 == 0` is ignored and does not corrupt stored reserves; both trading directions fire with the correct origin/destination; a late callback whose price has moved past `minAmountOut` reverts instead of filling at a loss; the kill switch stops both arms (and only the owner can flip it); and a stranger cannot call `react`, `onArb`, `runControl` or `setPaused`.
- **N-venue routing**: across three watched pools, the router picks the cheapest *executable* venue and refuses to route to a cheaper pool that has no executor; the constructor rejects mismatched chain/pool arrays, fewer than two venues, and duplicate chain IDs.
- **Failed-request handling**: when the system rejects a callback request, no round is consumed, the cooldown and in-flight window are untouched, no `GapDetected` is emitted (no phantom round), `CallbackFailed` is emitted, the price observation is kept, and the next tick retries with the same would-be round id.
